# AUDIENCE: who The Wealth Gap is for

Status: **working draft, 2026-11-11.** It is a hypothesis built from 6 uploads and a handful of comments, not a
finding. Revise it when the numbers say otherwise (see "Evidence so far" and "Open questions"). The idea checker
(`bun run ideas`, dashboard → Ideas) reads this file for every idea, so keep it short and concrete.

## The viewer in one sentence
An adult who earns and spends money but was never taught how the money system works, who suspects the game is
tilted toward people who already have money, and wants to see exactly how, in plain English, so they can make
better decisions with their own money.

## What they are like
- **Curious, not expert.** Has heard words like inflation, stop-loss, balance sheet, but could not explain them.
  Jargon is fine only if it is explained the moment it appears.
- **Feels it in their own life.** Rent, prices, savings, a brokerage app, a 401k or a pension. They care about a
  topic when it touches their wallet or their sense of fairness.
- **Skeptical of hype.** They don't want "get rich" tips, stock picks or gurus. They want the mechanism: who does
  what, why it works, and what it means for them.
- **Ages roughly 20 to 40 (assumption).** Old enough to have money decisions, young enough to feel behind. Older
  viewers watch too (the 64-year-old commenter), often to disagree, and that's fine.
- **English-speaking, mostly US data so far (assumption).** The videos use US numbers (BLS, median home price,
  401k). See open questions.

## The promise of the channel
Every video shows **one gap**: between what ordinary people are told and what is actually happening, or between how
insiders and everyone else play the same game. It explains the mechanism with sourced numbers and ends on what it
means for the viewer. The name is the brief: *the wealth gap*, and how it is made.

## The idea test (the checker scores each one)
An idea fits when it passes most of these:
1. **A gap.** Is there a "what you're told vs what's true" or "insiders vs you" angle? (Not just "how X works".)
2. **Their wallet.** Can the viewer connect it to their own money in the first 30 seconds?
3. **Plain English.** Can it be explained to a curious beginner without losing them, with one strong visual metaphor?
4. **Provable.** Can every key claim be sourced (official data, filings, papers)? No speculation dressed as fact.
5. **A package a stranger clicks.** Can you write a title (30–60 characters) and a one-object thumbnail that makes
   someone who has never heard of the channel curious? If you can't, drop or rework the idea.
6. **Not a repeat.** Does it add something new beyond what the channel already published? A sequel to a topic that
   did well is fine; a third take on one that did badly is not.
7. **Lasts.** Will it still be true and interesting in a year? News hooks are fine as the opening, not as the whole video.

## Out of scope
Trading signals and "my strategy" content, stock or crypto picks, get-rich-quick, personal budgeting tips with no
system angle, pure news recaps, politics for its own sake (policy is in scope only through its effect on money).

## Evidence so far (update after each analytics refresh)
As of 2026-11-08, 11 subscribers, 4 long videos and 3 shorts:
- Best long video: "Why You'll Never Own a Home (Not Your Fault)": the most views (139), the best CTR (2.3%), 2 subs,
  and a long comment arguing back. Personal stake plus a "not your fault" gap.
- Stop-loss / stop-hunt (trading microstructure): the weakest long videos (42 and 73 views), but the best shorts
  (615 and 335 views). It works as a fast hook; as a 10-minute video it may be too niche or too close to trading content.
- AI-bubble accounting (VID06, 16.6 min, testing a longer format): too new to judge.
- Click rates of 1.5–2.3% on long videos: packaging is the main problem, not retention (44–61% average viewed).

## Open questions (answer these as data comes in)

- Personal-finance stakes (housing, prices, savings) vs market mechanics (stops, accounting): which brings
  subscribers? Track subs per 1,000 views by topic.
- Do 15-minute videos keep watch time up, or do 8–10 minutes do better? (VID06 is the test.)
