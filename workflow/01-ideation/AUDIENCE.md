# AUDIENCE: who The Wealth Gap is for

Status: **working brief, revised 2026-11-13** from the creator's channel blueprint, checked against the channel's own
numbers (9 uploads, 27 subscribers on 2026-11-11). It is still a hypothesis: revise it when the numbers say otherwise
(see "Evidence so far" and "Open questions"). The idea finder and checker (`bun run ideas`, dashboard → Ideas), the
package concepts, the thumbnail review and the publish metadata all read this file, so keep it short and concrete.

## Niche and angle
**Financial reality and market mechanics.** The channel breaks down the hidden mechanics of money, banking and the
stock market so ordinary people can see how the financial game is actually played.

The angle: **what the system does vs. what regular people are told.** The channel is the investigative guide who
pulls back the curtain on how banks, institutions and markets work, with evidence. It is not a get-rich-quick channel,
not a dry economics lecture, and not a conspiracy channel: every claim is sourced and the mechanism is shown.

## The viewer in one sentence
A working adult of 20 to 40 who knows the words (shares, interest rates, inflation, banks) but not the plumbing behind
them, who feels the system is stacked against people like them and wants evidence and a clear explanation, not a
conspiracy theory, so they can protect and grow their own money.

## What they are like
- **Intermediate beginner.** Has a bank account, a salary, maybe an investing app or a retirement account. Knows the
  vocabulary, can't explain the mechanism. Jargon is fine only if it is explained the moment it appears.
- **Working professionals, graduates, retail investors, savers**, roughly 20 to 40. Old enough to have real money
  decisions, young enough to feel behind. Older viewers watch too, often to argue back; that's fine.
- **Skeptical of standard advice** ("work hard, keep it in a savings account"), and of hype and gurus. They want the
  mechanism: who does what, why it works, what it costs them.
- **Busy and on a phone.** No patience for a slow intro or someone rambling over a chart. They stay for focused
  storytelling, clear visuals and the "aha, it wasn't my imagination" moment.
- **English-speaking; the videos use US data so far** (assumption, see open questions).

### What keeps them up at night
- "Why does working hard feel like a treadmill where everything keeps getting more expensive?"
- "Why do I always buy at the top and sell at the bottom?"
- "Is my money actually safe in the bank, and what does the bank do with it?"
- "How do wealthy people legally grow their net worth during downturns while I fall behind?"

## The promise of the channel
Every video shows **one gap**: between what ordinary people are told and what actually happens, or between how
insiders and everyone else play the same game. It explains the mechanism with sourced numbers, translates it into
money for one ordinary person, and ends on what it means for the viewer. The name is the brief: *the wealth gap*,
and how it is made.

## Content pillars
Every video sits in one pillar. Rotate between them so the channel doesn't become one topic.

1. **The banking and monetary system (money).** What banks do with deposits, how money is created, debt cycles,
   interest-rate spreads, inflation and why savings lose purchasing power.
2. **Wealth and asset ownership (the gap).** Why assets (housing, index funds, commodities) outrun wages, who owns
   them, and how ordinary people can protect what they earn. Housing lives here.
3. **Market mechanics and retail traps (stocks).** How institutions, liquidity and hype cycles catch everyday
   investors: buying tops, fees, index-fund flows, how a bubble pulls savers in.

**Pillar 3 caution from the channel's numbers:** long videos on trading microstructure (stop hunts, inducement
traps) got 77–78 views each, while their shorts got 337–635. As a long video, a market-mechanics topic must be about
the viewer's own savings, retirement account or the app they invest with; pure trader topics go to shorts.

## The idea test (the checker scores each one)
An idea fits when it passes most of these:
1. **A pillar.** Does it clearly sit in one of the three pillars? Day-trading tips, penny stocks and celebrity net
   worth don't belong.
2. **A gap and a mechanism.** Is there a "what you're told vs what's true" or "insiders vs you" angle, and does the
   video explain *why* it happens (a system), not just *that* it happened? "How X works" alone is not enough.
3. **Their wallet in 15 seconds.** Would an everyday saver or investor care within the first 15 seconds? Can the
   main number be said as money for one person ("on $10,000 in savings, that's $X a year")? Frame the stake
   ("What your bank does with your paycheck"), not the topic ("How banks work").
4. **Plain English.** Can a curious beginner follow it, with one strong visual metaphor and a character who stands
   in for the viewer?
5. **Provable.** Can every key claim be sourced (official data, filings, papers)? Evidence, not conspiracy, and no
   speculation dressed as fact. Watch for hooks that are punchy but wrong (comparing a savings rate with a
   credit-card rate as if it were "the same dollar").
6. **A package a stranger clicks.** Can you write a title (30–60 characters) and a one-object thumbnail that make
   someone who has never heard of the channel curious? If you can't, drop or rework the idea.
7. **Not a repeat.** Does it add something new beyond what the channel already published? A sequel to a topic that
   did well is fine; a third take on one that did badly is not.
8. **Lasts.** Will it still be true and interesting in a year? News hooks are fine as the opening, not as the whole
   video.

## Out of scope
Trading signals and "my strategy" content, stock or crypto picks, day trading, penny stocks, get-rich-quick, celebrity
net worth, personal budgeting tips with no system angle, pure news recaps, fear content with no evidence ("pull your
money out now"), politics for its own sake (policy is in scope only through its effect on money).

## Evidence so far (update after each analytics refresh)
As of 2026-11-11: 27 subscribers, 4 long videos and 5 shorts in the snapshot (VID08 bank savings came later).
- **Best long video by far:** "Why You'll Never Own a Home (Not Your Fault)" (pillar 2): 1,019 views, 2.2% click
  rate, 49% average viewed, 81% still watching at 0:30. Most views came from suggested videos (130 of the tracked
  ones). Personal stake plus a "not your fault" gap.
- **Weakest long videos:** stop-loss / stop-hunt / inducement (trading microstructure): 77–78 views, 1.6–2.2% click
  rate, mostly from subscribers (YouTube did not push them to strangers). Their shorts did well (337 and 635 views).
- **AI-bubble accounting** (VID06, 16.6 min): 58 views, but its short "How Big Tech Looks More Profitable Without
  Earning More" got 1,149 views, the channel's best short. The topic interests people; the long package didn't land.
- **Where viewers leave:** the steepest loss is in the first 30–60 s, which is also where the first chapter card
  sits. Later chapter cards show no extra drop on the one video with a real sample (VID03). So: no chapter card in the
  first minute and a half; later ones are fine when the previous line leaves a question open.
- **Click rates of 1.5–2.3%** on long videos: packaging (topic, title, thumbnail) is the main problem.
