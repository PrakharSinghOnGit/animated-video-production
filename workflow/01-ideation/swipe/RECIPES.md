# Proven packages: what 1,031 finance outliers have in common

Source: `outliers.json` (46 channels, videos at 2x or more of their channel's local median, collected 2026-11-17; method and limits in `README.md`, full write-up in `workflow/07-publish/GUIDE.md` section 12).
These are correlations, not causes. A recipe is a structure to adapt and combine, never a thumbnail or title to copy.

## What decides an outlier
- **The topic and promise, not the style.** Within one channel, outliers and ordinary videos look the same (same word count, faces, colours, brightness). A channel's look is constant; what beats its median is what the package promises.
- **Your money beats the news.** Title words with the most outliers (lift over the 18.9% base rate): *per month* 2.3x, *credit card(s)* 1.9-2.3x, *explains* 2.2x, *guide / beginners* 1.9-2.1x, a `$` figure 1.5-1.9x, *paid / pay* 1.7-1.8x, *house* 1.8x, *index funds* 1.7x, *fake / fraud* 1.6-1.7x, a famous investor by name 1.6-2x, *everyone* 1.7x. Fewest: *news* 0.2x, *markets / rate* 0.3x, *today* 0.3x, *Fed* 0.3x, *stocks* 0.4x, *inflation* 0.5x, *breaking* 0.5x. So: show what the system does to the viewer's money (a sum per month, their card, their house, a fraud done to people like them); do not report a macro event.
- **Title form** (small effects): a `$` figure 1.46x, colon or dash framing 1.21x, "How ..." 1.21x, a superlative 1.31x; worse: `!` or emoji 0.73x, a question mark 0.82x, 40 characters or fewer 0.78x.
- **Faceless outliers** (267 with no face): one subject, centred (60%), about 4 words, a label bar or highlighter behind the words, curiosity (54%) or worry (19%) carried by the subject.

## Ten faceless recipes (name them in `recipe`; combining two is encouraged)
1. `number-hero`: one huge figure is the image, set on or beside its physical object (a cheque, a banknote, a coin, a bill). The figure is the largest element.
2. `logo-twist`: a familiar brand drawn as an object (its box, card, sign, storefront) plus 1-3 words that turn it into a story.
3. `defaced-icon`: an innocent everyday object with one wrong detail (glowing eyes, a red X, a crack, a stain). The wrongness is the hook.
4. `strike-through`: a number crossed out and replaced by a worse or better one (years left, price then and now).
5. `one-red-country`: a map with one country (or state) filled in the accent ink and 1-2 words.
6. `character-vs-arrow`: the channel's character reacting to one thing happening to it, large: a falling arrow, a bill, a closing door. Penny's `worried` / `surprised` moods, the Agent's pose.
7. `this-vs-that`: two halves, one marked right and one wrong, or two things with only a `<` or `>` between them.
8. `highlighted-document`: a headline, letter, statement or receipt with one phrase in highlighter or circled in red.
9. `hidden-hand`: a hand (gloved, shadowed, inked) taking money or holding the viewer's thing, with a `?` or no words: an implied actor without a face.
10. `scale-shot`: something vast seen from above (a car lot, a warehouse, a queue) with a 2-4 word label bar giving the stake.

## How to borrow
- Borrow the **structure** (a recipe, a title shape like "How X Really Makes Money" or "$X Per Month"), then fill it with this video's subject, its sourced facts and the house style.
- Never reuse another video's image, its exact words, or its exact pairing of subject + words + layout. The concept check rejects a title that shares a run of 5 words with an outlier.
- Logos only to name the company the video is about, never so the package looks made by or endorsed by it.
